# T46 · Notify: subscriptions, the loop that evaluates them, the poster, and every notice's words

## Outcome
`notify::Notifier` holds subscriptions in memory, evaluates each after every applied refresh with the waiters' own `condition::evaluate`, ends it by NTF-4's table, and posts its one notice through a `Poster`; `RealPoster` writes NTF-6's two lines to a session's socket; `notify::text` holds every notice's words, fixed by golden files; and `stop` tells every session when the daemon stops in order (NTF-10).

## Context
T02 declared everything in `plan/contracts/notify.md` under `src/notify/`, with `NoNotify` real and the rest stubs. T15's store has the `Subscribed` guard, `changed()` and `View`; T44 built `condition::evaluate`; T10 built `render::outcome_line`, `flags`, `duration` and `stale_mark`; T09 built `cadence::stale`; T04 built `SleepWatch` and `Warnings::since`; T43 built `FakePoller`, which your tests use for `PollerHandle::status`. This task fills the leaf. **Nothing here knows HTTP or GitHub:** the route that registers (T47) hands over a `NewSubscription` it has already checked and evaluated once; the daemon's wiring (T28) spawns the notifier and calls `stop`; the CLI (T48) uses `text::watching` for its `registered:` line. Everything that posts is a (proposal): the format is known from Claude Code's debug log, on Linux only (docs/07).

## Scope
- **A subscription (NTF-1, NTF-9).** What `NewSubscription` holds, plus its `Subscribed` guard, `expires_at` and its place in the order registered. In memory only: no file, no `Serialize`. `SessionToken`: no `Display`, no `Serialize`, a `Debug` that prints `SessionToken(…)`; `Deserialize` from a string.
- **`NotifyHandle` for `Notifier` (NTF-2's and NTF-3's share here: the registry; the routes are T47's).** `register`, `drop_same`, `cancel`, `count` and `look`, each exactly as `plan/contracts/notify.md`, "Semantics", says: the order inside `register`; "the same request" compared as the daemon reads it (NTF-2): the same `socket`, the PR by node ID, `until`, `sha` lower-cased as given (a short and a full SHA differ), `fail_fast` with left out as `false`, and `kind` as a set; a replacement takes no new place; `state::MAX_SUBSCRIPTIONS` in all and `PER_SOCKET` for one socket otherwise; the confirmation posted once, inside `clock::timeout(POST_LIMIT)`, and nothing kept unless it's `Done`, the replaced one staying gone. `cancel` matches the node ID or the name registered under (NTF-1, NTF-3).
- **The three states (NTF-2, NTF-6):** confirming, open, telling, as the contract says. All three count, hold their guard and have their socket checked at the tick; an end found while confirming waits for the confirmation, and is dropped with it if that fails. A subscription leaves, and drops its guard, when its notice's post has ended, however it ended (that drop is STA-1's end, T15's). **There's no state in which a notice waits to be tried again** (NTF-6, NTF-10).
- **The loop (NTF-4).** One task. It wakes on `store.changed()`, on a registration, on `sleep.subscribe()`, and every `TICK` while a subscription is held. Each wake: one `store.view()`, then every confirming and open subscription in the order registered, the first that applies ending it:
  1. `condition::evaluate`: an outcome is `Satisfied` or `Ended(reason)`.
  2. On a tick, `poster.look(socket)`: `Missing`, or another `SocketId` than `socket_id`, is `SessionGone`, nothing posted. `Failed` changes nothing, and is logged once for that subscription.
  3. On a tick, `problem` (NTF-4, POL-26): `warnings.since(PrFailing, <node ID>)` is `Some`, so one registered while its PR has `pr_failing` raised posts at the first tick; or `poller.status().problem` is `auth` and `PROBLEM_AFTER` has passed on the monotonic clock since `poller.status().problem_since`: **from when `auth` began, not from when this loop saw it**, and a failed try with a new token doesn't restart it.
  4. On a tick, `for`, by the waiter's rule (`clock.md`, "Deadlines that count sleep"): never the wall clock. **Held** instead of expired when first found passed at a wake where `sleep.total()` moved, or while the record is stale (`cadence::stale`): it expires at the first wake after its PR's next applied refresh (`timing.fetched` moved) if step 1 gave nothing, or after `CATCH_UP`, as it is.
  The ended subscription's notice is built from that same view: `text::outcome`, `text::problem`, or `text::expired` with the record's `status` and `render::stale_mark` (the record's `stale` set by `cadence::stale` with `poller.status().tier`).
- **Notices on their way (NTF-6).** Each goes out on its own, apart from the loop and from the engine: first a `look` (the path checked as at a tick; `Failed` doesn't stop it), then `post` inside `clock::timeout(POST_LIMIT)`. **Whatever it gives, the subscription leaves, and nothing is tried again** (NTF-6). `Done`: never sent again. `SessionGone`, `Failed` (a full queue's `EAGAIN` included), or the limit passing before `post` returned: logged as `notice not posted` with `reason=` `session_gone`, `failed` or `timeout`. A slow post holds no other.
- **`stop(asked)` and `abort` (NTF-10).** `stop`: from its first instant the registry is closed (`register` answers `Stopping`, `count()` 0), and everything held is posted **all at once**, with `STOP_LIMIT` for all of them together, nothing tried again: `text::stopped(told, asked)` to the open ones, and to the confirming ones once their confirmation was written; a telling one's post already under way is awaited in the same `STOP_LIMIT`, in place of `stopped`. It returns when every post has ended or the limit has passed. Whatever wasn't written by then is logged in one line, also when the future is dropped: do that from a guard's `Drop`. `abort`: the loop ends, everything is dropped, nothing is posted or logged.
- **`RealPoster` (NTF-6, OPS-16's share here: the one connection besides GitHub's).** `look`: `tokio::fs::metadata`; `NotFound` is `Missing`, another error `Failed`; a socket (`FileTypeExt::is_socket`) whose `uid` is the process's effective one is `Socket`, anything else `Other` naming what it is; `SocketId` from `dev` and `ino`. `post`: `tokio::net::UnixStream::connect`; with a token, the auth line; the message line; return `Done`. The two lines are built with `serde_json` and written with `write_all`, each ending in `\n`. `NotFound`, `ConnectionRefused` and `PermissionDenied` are `SessionGone`; any other error `Failed`; the text is the `io::Error`'s. **Nothing is read, and nothing but those two lines is ever written**: no `from`, no mode, no address.
- **`notify::text` (NTF-7, NTF-8).** The seven functions of the contract, pure. `watching` is NTF-7's one rule for the condition's words, for every parameter: the confirmation and T48's `registered:` line both use it. The outcome line, the flags, the durations and the stale mark are T10's functions, called. `ask_again` puts `--for` in whole seconds.
- **`fake::RecordingPoster`**, as the contract's fake section says, under `cfg(any(test, feature = "test-endpoint"))`.
- **Logging (OPS-9's part "Logged", this task's rows):** `subscription registered pr=… until=…`, `subscription ended pr=… end=…` with `End::as_str` (and `why=` for `problem`), `notice not posted pr=… reason=…`, `socket not checked pr=…` once. Never the text, a token or a socket's path.
- **Work in flight (clock.md).** The loop holds a `Busy` from waking until it parks again. A post and a `look` hold one while they run: real I/O that ends without the clock moving. **That's TST-4's "notices being posted"**: a post holds its `Busy` from its `look` until it ends, except under `RecordingPoster::stall`, which excuses it. Waiting for a held expiry's refresh is parked.
- **TST-7's share:** every notice of NTF-7 and NTF-8 as a golden file in `tests/golden/notify/`. The same texts as a session really gets them are T35's.

## Traps
- **Your own shell has a real session socket.** `CLAUDE_CODE_MESSAGING_SOCKET` is set in the session that runs you. Nothing here reads the environment, and no test posts anywhere but to a `RecordingPoster` or a listener it bound: a line written to the real one lands in a live session.
- **`post` awaits nothing after the last byte.** The caller's timeout decides "written in full" by whether `post` returned (the contract). A `shutdown().await` or a `flush().await` after the write breaks that: drop the stream instead.
- **A post can't be made to hang with a real socket:** a peer that doesn't read takes the line. The 2 s and the 1 s are tested with `RecordingPoster::stall`, which excuses the stalled post; a real post holds its `Busy`.
- **Mark the watch seen before you evaluate** (`borrow_and_update`), then await `changed()`: the other order loses a refresh applied in between.
- **`changed()` moves for every PR, and on an unchanged refresh too.** Evaluate every subscription on each wake; don't try to guess whose refresh it was.
- **No lock across an `.await`.** `register` awaits the confirmation: take what the post needs out of the registry's lock first. `cancel`, `drop_same` and `count` are plain functions.
- **Drop the guard when the subscription leaves, never at its end.** The guard keeps the PR's record (STA-1) and its drop starts the PR's 60 minutes; a notice still being posted counts.
- **`for` never reads the wall clock.** `expires_at` is only what the answer reports; the deadline is the monotonic time plus the sleep noticed, as a waiter's (`clock.md`).
- **A held expiry needs the PR's own refresh**, not any `changed()`: compare `timing.fetched` with its value when it was held.
- A JSON string escapes a newline by itself; never build the line with `format!`.
- The tick runs only while a subscription is open: a notifier with none must not keep a timer, or every test's clock fires it for nothing.
- The `Debug` of `NewSubscription` and of anything that holds a `SessionToken` goes through `SessionToken`'s: don't write a `Debug` by hand that prints fields.

## Open questions
- **Everything that posts is a (proposal)**, and only Linux is measured (docs/07): the two lines, that nothing answers, the three "session gone" errors. Built as NTF-6 writes it; one function builds each line, so a format change is one edit.
- **Plan decision P15, built:** the tick is this loop's own timer, with POL-26's period; `ask_again` puts `--for` before `--since`.
- **A session that was killed may leave its socket file** (NTF-4, not measured): then the tick sees nothing wrong, and the subscription lasts until its notice is refused or `for` passes.
- **NTF-4's `problem` for `auth` is timed from `PollerStatus::problem_since`** (T21 publishes it), so it comes at the first tick at or after the 10 minutes. `pr_failing` is T42's warning, read with `Warnings::since`.
- `pr_failing`'s `problem` notice is NTF-8's sentence with its code (D9), and a forward step of the wall clock counts towards `for` as a sleep (NTF-4, D9): both are in the spec now.
